## Slide 6

Título: "GENERAL LEDGER ACCOUNTING". Texto: "Now, consider this example: GBI purchases $1,000 of office supplies, but on credit rather than with cash. In this case, the purchase and payment are recorded separately. One account is supplies expense, which is debited by $1,000. The offsetting account is payables-miscellaneous, which is credited by $1,000. Payment is made using the bank account ($1,000 credit). Finally, the payables-miscellaneous account is cleared ($1,000 debit)."

**Figura 4**: diagrama de 3 cuentas en T con numeración de pasos (①=Purchase, ②=Payment):

- **Supplies expense** (saldo 740000): ① Debit 1,000 | Credit (vacío)
- **Bank account** (saldo 100000): Debit (vacío) | Credit 1,000 ②

Secuencia: (1) Purchase → debita Supplies expense y acredita Payables-Miscellaneous por 1,000; (2) Payment → debita Payables-Miscellaneous y acredita Bank account por 1,000.

## Slide 7

Título: "GENERAL LEDGER ACCOUNTING". Texto: "If a company purchases supplies from multiple sources or vendors, it records all the payable data in one account, payables-miscellaneous. One problem with this arrangement is that the company cannot determine how much money it owes to each vendor. If the company needs to track the money it owes to individual vendors, then it uses a separate process known as accounts payable accounting, which utilizes subledger accounts. Accounts payable accounting is typically used in conjunction with the procurement process."

Visual: foto en blanco y negro de calculadora, lapiceros y hojas con columnas de números financieros — decorativa.

## Slide 8

Título: "SUBSIDIARY LEDGERS AND RECONCILIATION ACCOUNTS". Texto: "Some financial data are not directly maintained in the general ledger. Customer accounts, which track the amounts customers owe and the payments they have made, are maintained separately for each customer. Although it is necessary to track sales and payments separately for each customer, it is not necessary to include each customer account in the general ledger. Similarly, data about each vendor is maintained in a separate account. Vendor accounts track purchases from and payments made to them. Such accounts are maintained in subsidiary ledgers or subledgers, and they are not part of the general ledger."

Visual: foto en blanco y negro de una laptop MacBook sobre una mesa/silla con libreta y marco de fotos — decorativa.

## Slide 9

Título: "SUBSIDIARY LEDGERS AND RECONCILIATION ACCOUNTS". Texto: "Although customer and vendor accounts are not part of the general ledger, the data in these accounts must be reflected in the general ledger. Companies accomplish this task by posting the data from subledger accounts into special accounts in the general ledger called reconciliation accounts. Reconciliation accounts are general ledger accounts that consolidate data from a group of related subledger accounts, such as customers and vendors. The reconciliation account for customers is accounts receivable, and the reconciliation account for vendors is accounts payable."

Visual: foto en blanco y negro de una persona escribiendo en una libreta, con monedas y una alcancía sobre el escritorio — decorativa.

## Slide 10

Título: "SUBSIDIARY LEDGERS AND RECONCILIATION ACCOUNTS". Texto: "One special characteristic of reconciliation accounts is that it is not possible to post data directly into them. Rather, data must be posted to subledger accounts, at which point they are automatically posted to the corresponding reconciliation account as well. Thus, when a company sells products or services to a customer on credit, the amount owed is noted in the customer's subledger account and is also posted to the corresponding reconciliation account (accounts receivable). Likewise, when the company owes money to a vendor for purchases it made on credit, this amount is noted in the vendor's subledger account and is simultaneously posted to the corresponding reconciliation account (accounts payable). The balance in the reconciliation account (e.g., accounts receivable and accounts payable) is the sum of the postings in the related subledger accounts (e.g., customers and vendors, respectively)."

Visual: foto en blanco y negro de dos personas trabajando con laptop, calculadora numérica y gráficos impresos sobre la mesa — decorativa.

## Slide 11

Título: "ACCOUNTS PAYABLE (AP) ACCOUNTING". Texto: "Organizations use subledgers to track money owed to individual vendors. Subledgers are not part of the general ledger but instead are associated with special accounts in the general ledger known as reconciliation accounts. The subledger account number is created when the vendor master record is created."

Diagrama "Accounts Payable Accounting" (flujo de subledgers → cuenta de reconciliación → general ledger, con pasos numerados):

**Accounts payable subledger accounts** (3 cuentas en T):
- Vendor 1: ④ Debit 2,000 | Credit 2,000 ①
- Vendor 2: ⑤ Debit 1,000 | Credit 1,000 ②
- Vendor 3: ⑥ Debit 4,000 | Credit 4,000 ③

**General ledger accounts**:
- Accounts payable reconciliation (saldo 300700): Debit 7,000 | Credit 7,000
- Supplies expense (saldo 740000): Debit ① 2,000, ② 1,000, ③ 4,000 | Credit (vacío)
- Bank (saldo 100000): Debit (vacío) | Credit ④ 2,000, ⑤ 1,000, ⑥ 4,000

Leyenda de pasos: ① Purchase from vendor 1, ② Purchase from vendor 2, ③ Purchase from vendor 3, ④ Payment to vendor 1, ⑤ Payment to vendor 2, ⑥ Payment to vendor 3.

Las flechas curvas del diagrama conectan cada subledger de Vendor con la cuenta de reconciliación (Accounts payable reconciliation) y con las cuentas del general ledger (Supplies expense / Bank): cada compra (①②③) se debita en Supplies expense y se credita en el vendor + reconciliación; cada pago (④⑤⑥) se debita en el vendor + reconciliación y se acredita en Bank.

Título: "ACCOUNTS RECEIVABLE (AR) ACCOUNTING".
